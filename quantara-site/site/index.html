02 / the refusals

a values page is adjectives. this is a list with a cost.

you can tell what an ai company is by reading its customer list, not its ethics page. here is ours, in the negative.

01

no pane of glass

we won't build the vantage point that watches many people at once. keys change hands; architecture doesn't.

02

no risk scoring of human beings

no predictive policing, no recidivism models, no pre-crime, no threat classification of individuals.

03

no biometric registries

no face, iris, gait, or voice enrollment. not for onboarding, not for fraud, not for "proof of personhood." not for anything.

04

no enforcement or targeting

nothing for immigration enforcement, nothing for battlefields, nothing that feeds the carceral pipeline.

05

no data brokerage

we don't sell, rent, or aggregate user data. in most of what we build, there is no dataset to sell at all.

06

no telemetry by default

logging is off unless you turn it on. deletion is a function you call, not a form you submit.

07

no training on you

no model of ours learns from user data without explicit, revocable, per-use consent.

08

no contract we can't disclose

if we can't tell you who we're working for, we aren't working for them.

these are refusals, not preferences. we would rather lose the contract.

03 / the thinking

there is no singularity — only decisions being described as prophecy.

inevitability is a governance strategy, not a forecast. every "ai will inevitably…" is a request to stop asking who decided, on what budget, to what deadline. software has authors. name them and the spell breaks.

aug 31, 2026 seven silent failures 9 min → i built an autonomous email agent in five days. seven things broke, and six of them broke without saying anything — a model that exists but not in that region, a dedupe lock guarding the wrong door, and a container with no idea what timezone it was in. aug 23, 2026 six runs, one model 7 min → shipping an 8b open-weights security model, and everything that went wrong on the way — a model that wouldn't stop talking, three gates that passed while measuring nothing, and a benchmark bet that came out worse than the model i started from. aug 22, 2026 qwennie: a 78,672-parameter chat transformer running in pure CSS 6 min → a real decoder-only transformer — attention, rope, a kv cache made of inherited custom properties — compiled into an 8mb stylesheet. no javascript anywhere, and every reply is byte-identical to the numpy forward pass. aug 3, 2026 Tailwagging: A Design Strategy 10 min → the quantara identity derived from first principles: perimeter oscillations, the pup ratio, and the founding of puppygirl science. our most rigorous document. jul 29, 2026 The Myth of the Gentle Singularity 10 min → sam altman's essay, his company's incident report, and its balance sheet, read against each other. jul 28, 2026 The July 2026 Incident and the Need for Localized AI Defense 5 min → an agent escaped its sandbox and hit hugging face at machine speed. the defenders' first obstacle was their own safety filters. dec 13, 2025 Ouroboros: Optical Feedback in Autonomous Embedded Agents 3 min → a hardware-in-the-loop rig that lets a coding agent watch its own code run — and reset the board when it lies.
04 / the proof

the privacy claim is architectural. you can check it.

"we respect your privacy" is a promise, and every surveillance company makes it. these are facts about where the computation happens.

i.

inference

models execute on your cpu or gpu. there is no endpoint to intercept, bill, or log.

ii.

storage

files, prompts, and history stay on your disk. we can't produce under subpoena what we never had.

iii.

verification

the source is public. don't take our word for it — open the network tab and watch nothing happen.

05 / who's here

small, independent, building in the open.

quantara is not a large organization and doesn't pretend to be one. we'd rather be visibly small and honest than perform the scale of an institution. no investors to answer to; no growth curve that requires collecting anything.

the dominant model in this industry is centralization: gather everyone's data, route everything through one provider, and trust the intentions of whoever holds the keys this quarter. we think that's a bad architecture regardless of intentions — so we build things that structurally can't be turned around on the people using them.

small tools that run locally, do one thing, and end. if that's useful to you, take it. it's free.
